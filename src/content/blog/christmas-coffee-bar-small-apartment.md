## The rule: swap, do not add

A small coffee bar has room for what it already holds and nothing else. So every Christmas thing that goes on replaces something that comes off for the month. Two holiday mugs replace two everyday ones. A jar of cocoa replaces a jar of something you will not miss in December. One seasonal syrup replaces the others. The footprint stays the same, the corner still works at seven in the morning, and putting it back in January takes ten minutes.

Pick two mugs for December and put two everyday ones in the box. ### 3. Greenery that does not need water

A few sprigs of faux pine or dried eucalyptus in a small jar or vase. Real cuttings dry out quickly in a warm kitchen and drop needles into everything, including the machine. Faux greenery goes back in the box with the lights.

### 4. Cocoa in jars, not boxes

Decant the cocoa mix, mini marshmallows and crushed peppermint into glass jars. A row of jars with their contents showing is the decoration, and it makes the shelf look planned. Two or three jars is plenty. A fourth is clutter.

## Turn it into a hot cocoa bar for guests

A hot cocoa bar sounds like a party table, but in a small apartment it is just the tray from idea six, set out on the table.

What goes on it:

- Cocoa mix, or good dark chocolate chopped small
- A jug of hot milk
- Mini marshmallows and crushed peppermint in jars
- Whipped cream, kept in the fridge until guests arrive
- Cinnamon, and the peppermint syrup
- Mugs, spoons and napkins

Set it out, show people the frother once, and let them build their own. When the evening is over, everything that lives on the shelf goes back on the shelf. Nothing needs its own storage.

## What I would skip

**A separate cocoa cart or second station.** In a small apartment it is one more thing to walk around, and it gets used three times a year.

**Real candles next to the machine.** Steam, a kettle and an open flame on the same strip of counter is a bad mix. Warm lights on a timer give the same glow.

**Fresh garland near the coffee.** Needles end up in the mugs and the machine, and it dries out fast.

**A set of ten holiday mugs.** Swap two, do not add ten. They will need somewhere to live for the other eleven months.

**Signs with words on them.** On a shelf this size, every object competes with something useful. A sign is the first thing to cut.

## FAQ

**How do I make a Christmas coffee bar in a small space?**

Keep the footprint you already have and swap items rather than adding them. Warm lights on the shelf edge, two holiday mugs, cocoa in a glass jar and one seasonal syrup make it feel like Christmas without taking any more counter.

**What do you put on a hot cocoa bar?**

Cocoa mix or chopped chocolate, hot milk, mini marshmallows, crushed peppermint, whipped cream and a little cinnamon. A pump of peppermint syrup and a frother make it feel like a cafe. All of it fits on one tray.

**When should I set up a Christmas coffee bar?**

Usually in the weeks around Thanksgiving, and back down in the first week of January. If you swap rather than add, setting it up takes about fifteen minutes.

**How do I store it after the holidays?**

In one box: the lights, the ribbon, the faux greenery and the two holiday mugs wrapped in a tea towel. If it does not fit in one box, that is a sign you added instead of swapped.

## If you only change one thing

Add warm lights to the shelf edge. Not because they are the most important object on the shelf, but because they change how everything else on it looks, especially at seven on a December morning. The mugs, the jars and the syrup are details. The light is the mood.
